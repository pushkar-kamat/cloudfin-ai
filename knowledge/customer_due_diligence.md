# Customer Due Diligence Policy

## Purpose
Customer Due Diligence (CDD) helps the organization understand who the customer is, why the relationship is being established, and whether additional review is appropriate.

## Scope
CDD applies to new customers, beneficial owners, periodic reviews, material profile changes, and higher-risk events.

## Definitions
**Standard due diligence** is the normal level of review for ordinary risk. **Enhanced due diligence (EDD)** is additional review applied when risk factors justify deeper understanding or evidence.

## Procedures
CDD includes verifying customer identity, identifying beneficial ownership for entities, understanding the purpose and intended nature of the relationship, recording expected activity, and completing risk screening. Information should be refreshed when it becomes materially outdated.

## Requirements
The depth of due diligence should be proportionate to documented risk. Higher-risk situations may require additional source-of-funds or source-of-wealth information, management approval, or more frequent review. CDD records must be sufficiently clear for a reviewer to understand the basis of the assessment.

## Responsibilities
Onboarding teams collect information. Compliance defines risk factors and EDD requirements. Relationship owners communicate material customer changes. Review teams update due-diligence records when required.

## Escalation and Exceptions
Unverifiable ownership, unexplained high-risk connections, sanctions concerns, refusal to provide mandatory information, or serious inconsistencies must be escalated to Compliance before the relationship proceeds.
